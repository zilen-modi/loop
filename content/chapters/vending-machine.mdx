The vending machine is the classic interview build for the <Term id="state-machine">state machine</Term>. It's small enough to finish in an hour, and it rewards one decision above all: make the machine's *state* decide which actions are allowed, instead of scattering checks through every method.

## One object per state

The tempting design checks conditions everywhere: `if (balance > 0 && !maintenance && …)`. Each new requirement adds a condition to several methods, and one is always forgotten. Instead, give each state an object that answers the questions: may a customer act now? May the staff? Then each method starts with one check. When maintenance mode arrives as the extension, it's one new state object, and nothing else changes. That's the state pattern in its lightest form. If states need different *behaviour*, not just permissions, move the methods onto the state objects themselves.

## Money: escrow, then commit

Two rules keep the money right.

- **Hold inserted coins aside** (escrow) until a sale or refund. A refund then gives back exactly what went in, not "₹10 in some coins".
- **Check everything before changing anything.** A sale validates the slot, the stock and the balance, works out the change on a *copy* of the coin box, and only then commits the coins, the stock and the sale at once. A failed sale leaves no trace.

## Change, and why greedy can fail

The machine pays change from the coins it actually holds. The usual rule is greedy: the largest coin that fits, again and again. For ₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10 and ₹20 with unlimited coins, greedy always uses the fewest coins. With a *limited* coin box it can fail even when another mix would work:

Real machines handle this by refusing the sale and lighting EXACT CHANGE ONLY, and that's what the bench expects. If the interviewer asks for any valid mix, that's a small dynamic program over the coin counts. Mention it, and don't build it unless asked.

## In interviews

What you'll usually be asked next:

- "Card payments." A payment state with approve and decline events, and a timeout.
- "Give up after two minutes." A timer event on has-money that refunds automatically.
- "Find change with any valid mix." Dynamic programming over amounts with bounded coin counts.
- "Tell staff what to refill." Report empty slots and low coin counts.

Traps that cost people the round:

- if-chains in every method instead of states, and a forgotten case.
- Refunding "an amount" instead of the coins that went in.
- Changing stock or coins before knowing the sale will succeed.
- Change that uses coins the machine doesn't have.
